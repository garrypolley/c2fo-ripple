C2FO–Ripple ledger
Announcement Judgment Ledger Tape Questions

Unofficial · not C2FO, not Ripple

The partnership is public. The payments are not labeled.

On 27 October 2026, C2FO and Ripple said financing for C2FO Instant Pay would settle on the XRP Ledger in RLUSD. This page checks that claim against the public ledger. It does not show Instant Pay volume. Nothing on the ledger currently says which payments, if any, belong to the program.

What the announcement says

The Ripple press note from Ripple Swell in New York says businesses will keep using C2FO for early payment. Instant payment is funded by participating capital providers and settled on the XRP Ledger, in RLUSD, so a supplier can be paid in seconds instead of waiting on bank rails. Invoice terms stay as agreed.

The note says RLUSD has more than $2.5 billion in market capitalization, with more than $1 billion issued on the XRP Ledger. It points businesses who want Instant Pay to a waitlist. It does not publish a wallet, a memo format, a payment-channel id, or a volume figure for the new product.

C2FO’s copy of the release puts the existing early-pay history at more than $525 billion of working capital. That history is not on this ledger. Instant Pay is the piece that would be. The release still points interested businesses to a waitlist.

What is worth watching

Worth doing

RLUSD on the XRP Ledger is the right asset. Ripple’s docs name one mainnet issuer, rMxCKbEDwqr76QuheSUMdEGf4B9xJ8m5De. A payment of RLUSD is a public Payment whose delivered amount uses that issuer. A new holder sets a trust line first. Those two facts are enough to watch the rail the announcement named.

Not enough to score the program

RLUSD is a general stablecoin. An indexer counted about 68,000 holding accounts. The largest named balances in a 28 October check were Binance, Bybit, and Uphold, plus unlabeled accounts. A search for an account named C2FO came back empty. A large RLUSD payment is a candidate, not a C2FO settlement.

A first pass at a monitor assumed four signatures: new hot wallets with million-dollar trust lines, bursts of payments during business hours, a standard memo such as an invoice hash, and payment channels. The first two will happen for exchanges, market makers, and any other RLUSD user. The memo format and the payment channels are not in the announcement. Treating them as the program would invent a signal.

Self-payments and offers are a trap. Traders set the amount field to an enormous limit and pay themselves. The number that matters is the delivered amount on a payment between two different accounts, and only when the transaction succeeded.

So this page keeps two things. A snapshot of the issuer, so the size of the public rail is visible. A live tape of every successful RLUSD payment from one account to another, with 10,000 and above marked large. A listen on 28 October turned up small deliveries and no payment of 100 RLUSD or more in the samples. The tape is whoever is using RLUSD. It is not an Instant Pay dashboard. Offers and batch trades stay off the tape, because their amount field is a limit.

The issuer, checked against the ledger

Outstanding RLUSD is the issuer’s gateway_balances obligation: how much RLUSD the issuer account currently owes other accounts. That is net still out, not the cumulative amount ever issued. The announcement’s “more than $1 billion issued” can sit above this number after redemptions.

What would change this page

A published settlement address, a published memo type, or a capital-provider wallet would let a later version mark matching payments as identified. Until then, the honest page is this one: the rail is visible, the program is not.
